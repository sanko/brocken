=encoding utf-8

=head1 NAME

Brocken::Jenny::Codegen::Wasm::Encodings - WebAssembly opcode constants

=head1 SYNOPSIS

    use Brocken::Jenny::Codegen::Wasm::Encodings qw[I32_CONST I64_ADD END_BLOCK];

    $code .= pack( 'C', I32_CONST );  $code .= $leb128($value);
    $code .= pack( 'C', I64_ADD );
    $code .= pack( 'C', END_BLOCK );

=head1 DESCRIPTION

WebAssembly's code section is a stream of single-byte opcodes followed by LEB128 operands, so unlike the other three
back ends this table is close to a literal transcription of the specification's opcode numbering. Almost every constant
here is the byte from the binary format, and the operands after it are what the code generator has to supply.

There are 120 of them, covering control flow, locals, memory access, constants, the full comparison and arithmetic set
for i32, i64, f32, and f64, and the conversions between them.

=head1 EXPORTS

All 120 are exported by default, through C<@EXPORT>, C<@EXPORT_OK>, and the C<:all> tag.

=head1 CONSTANTS

=head2 Control and variables

C<UNREACHABLE> is 0x00, C<BLOCK> is 0x02 and C<END_BLOCK> is 0x0B, the structured control instructions. C<BR> is 0x0C
and C<BR_IF> is 0x0D, which branch out of a block, and C<RETURN> is 0x0F, which leaves the function. C<CALL> is 0x10.

Note that C<BR> and C<BR_IF> branch to a C<block>, not to a label in the C-language sense. Structured control is the
whole difference between this and the other three back ends, and it is why a Brocken C<while> loop compiles to a
C<block> and C<br_if> rather than to a compare and a jump.

C<LOCAL_GET> is 0x20 and C<LOCAL_SET> is 0x21, the two ways to reach a local. There is no C<local.tee> and no
C<local.grow> in the table.

=head2 Memory

Loads and stores, each with a natural alignment and an offset after the opcode:

=over

=item * C<I32_LOAD>, C<I64_LOAD>, C<F32_LOAD>, C<F64_LOAD> are the full-width loads, 0x28 to 0x2B.

=item * C<I32_LOAD8_U>, C<I32_LOAD16_U>, C<I64_LOAD8_U>, C<I64_LOAD16_U>, C<I64_LOAD32_U> are the narrow loads,
0x2D to 0x35. The C<_U> is a part of the instruction name, not decoration: WebAssembly has no sign-extending
narrow load, so loading a signed byte means a zero-extending load plus an explicit extend.

=item * C<I32_STORE>, C<I64_STORE>, C<F32_STORE>, C<F64_STORE> are 0x36 to 0x39, and C<I32_STORE8>, C<I32_STORE16>,
C<I64_STORE8>, C<I64_STORE16>, C<I64_STORE32> are 0x3A to 0x3E.

=back

=head2 Constants

C<I32_CONST> is 0x41, C<I64_CONST> is 0x42, C<F32_CONST> is 0x43, and C<F64_CONST> is 0x44. The first two take a signed
LEB128, the second pair takes a fixed-width IEEE bit pattern, which is why an f64 constant here is a byte swizzle
rather than a number.

=head2 Comparison

Eleven i32 comparisons from C<I32_EQZ> at 0x45 to C<I32_GE_U> at 0x4F, and the eleven i64 equivalents from C<I64_EQZ>
at 0x50 to C<I64_GE_U> at 0x5A. Each family has the S and U variants of the four orderings, because WebAssembly
compares integers as either signed or unsigned and says which.

The float comparisons are six per width, not eleven, from C<F32_EQ> at 0x5B to C<F32_GE> at 0x60 and C<F64_EQ> at 0x61
to C<F64_GE> at 0x66. There is no C<eqz> for a float: NaN compares false against everything including itself, which is
not what C<eqz> would mean.

=head2 Integer arithmetic

C<I32_ADD> through C<I32_SHR_U>, 0x6A to 0x76, and C<I64_ADD> through C<I64_SHR_U>, 0x7C to 0x88. Both families carry
the signed and unsigned divide and remainder variants.

Both are written with a C<_U> or C<_S> on the shifts as well, since a right shift has to know whether to bring in sign
bits.

=head2 Floating point

C<F32_ABS>, C<F32_NEG>, C<F32_COPYSIGN>, C<F32_SQRT>, then C<F32_ADD>, C<F32_SUB>, C<F32_MUL>, C<F32_DIV>, C<F32_MIN>,
and C<F32_MAX> from 0x8B to 0x97. The f64 equivalents run from 0x99 to 0xA5.

C<MIN> and C<MAX> are not C<add> and a comparison: in WebAssembly they follow IEEE 754 minimumNumber, which propagates
NaN. A Brocken program relying on C<fmin> to be a comparison has a different answer here than it would on x86-64.

=head2 Conversions

C<I32_WRAP_I64> is 0xA7, narrowing an i64 by discarding the top bits. C<I64_TRUNC_F64_S> is 0xA8, narrowing a float to
an integer and trapping if it does not fit. C<I64_EXTEND_I32_S> and C<I64_EXTEND_I32_U> are 0xAC and 0xAD, widening
with the sign or zero fill, and C<F64_CONVERT_I64_S> is 0xBB, integer to float.

The trap-on-overflow behaviour of the truncating conversions is worth knowing about: the other direction, the
saturating C<I64_TRUNC_SAT_F64_S> and the rounding C<I64_TRUNC_SAT_F64_S> family, are not in this table.

=head2 Value types

C<VALTYPE_I32> is 0x7F, C<VALTYPE_I64> is 0x7E, C<VALTYPE_F32> is 0x7D, and C<VALTYPE_F64> is 0x7C. These go in the
function signature and in a C<block>'s result type rather than in the instruction stream, and they count down from
0x7F, which is the specification's numbering.

There is no C<VALTYPE_V128>, because there is no vector type in the language.

=head1 STATUS

Expect breakage, and note that nothing can reach any of this yet.

The constructor in L<Brocken> has no WebAssembly branch, so C<Brocken-E<gt>new> dies with C<Unsupported platform> for a
wasm32 triple even though L<Brocken::Katsuro::Platform::Wasm> parses it and this module, along with
L<Brocken::Jenny::Lowerer::Wasm> and L<Brocken::Jenny::Linker::Wasm>, is in the tree. This is the most complete of the
four encoding tables and the least reachable, which is the odd shape of the project right now.

Even with a constructor, this table is not enough. A WebAssembly function needs its type section, and the imports and
exports that type has to agree with, and none of that is here. There is no C<i32.clz> or C<popcnt>, no C<rotl> or
C<rotr>, no bit-scan instructions, and the memory instructions all need an alignment hint and an offset that this
module does not carry.

The float C<MIN> and C<MAX> note above is not a gap but a real semantic difference between targets, and a program that
compares floats in a loop will not get the same answer compiled for wasm as it does natively.
